Notes from the field

Every order, every carrier, one dashboard. These notes are what our couriers learned on the road this quarter, one story at a time.

Read the story

The Tuesday that went sideways

A van, a bridge closure and forty parcels that still arrived before six. The dispatcher rerouted by hand while the map caught up, and the customers never knew.

What we changed: the map now reroutes first and asks questions later, and the dispatcher gets a single line to confirm instead of a form.

Why the estimate moved to 2pm

Orders placed before two ship the same day in every region we serve. The old cutoff was a guess; this one is measured, and it is honest about the edges.

The estimate on the order page reads the same clock the warehouse does, so the promise at checkout is the promise on the dock.

Two carriers, one label

The label printer no longer cares which carrier takes the parcel. The handoff scans the same code either way, and a late swap is a swap, not a reprint.

Returns follow the same rule in reverse: one code, any carrier, one place to look it up.

What the night shift sees

The dashboard at three in the morning is not the dashboard at noon. Fewer numbers, larger, and the ones that matter when nobody is around to explain them.

Every alert that fires at night has a sentence under it saying what to do, written by the person who last did it.

The parcel that went to the wrong Springfield

There are thirty-four of them. The address form now asks which one before the parcel leaves, and the map shows the answer so the person can see it is right.

It happened twice more that month, both times to the same street, and then never again.

Delays we caught before the customer did

Nine hundred and twelve this quarter. Each one got a message before the tracking page went stale, and most of them got a new estimate they could plan around.

The ones we did not catch are the ones we study on Fridays.

A carrier's page, watched

Can I watch a carrier's page instead of ours? Yes — any page the runner may open, scrolled or not, at any width the site serves.

What it reports is what a test would trip over: a link that goes somewhere else, a button that went, words that changed, things that overlap.

The end of the notes

Nothing below this line but the panel that breaks the page on purpose, so a monitor can be shown catching each break and recovering from it.

Every fault here is reversible, and Reset all puts the page back.
